About the studio

A research studio, design lab, and biocomputation consultancy

Assembloid Space is a research and design consultancy exploring synthetic ecologies of brain, machine, and motion. Founded by creative technologist and neuroarchitecture researcher Jenn Leung and bioethicist and functional genomics researcher Chloe Loewith, the studio operates at the intersection of cognition, computation, and embodiment.

Incubated at the Antikythera Cognitive Infrastructures Studio (2024) at the Berggruen Institute, the team investigates emerging cognitive systems across biological and computational substrates, contributing to the expanding field of Organoid Intelligence (OI) — the interfacing of brain organoids with digital systems to explore novel forms of adaptive, embodied computation.

Read our publication Organoid Array Computing: The Design Space of Organoid Intelligence in antikythera: journal for the philosophy of planetary computation.

What we do

Services

Assembloid Space works with universities, laboratories, cultural institutions, and technology companies as a research and consultancy partner. Our services fall into three areas:

Research & Development

We design intelligence architectures at the intersection of living neural systems and computational environments — developing the methodological and ethical frameworks for organoid intelligence scaffolding.

Consultancy & Advisory

Strategic research partners for organizations navigating the emerging landscape of biological computing — from technical implementation of organoid–silicon interfaces to the bioethical implications of designing for living intelligence, including knowledge mobilization and policy translation.

Platform & Tooling

Building the foundational tools and design principles for hybrid bio-computational systems — bridging synthetic biology, game engines, and philosophy to explore how biological intelligence perceives and acts in digital worlds.

Open-source tooling

UE-CL1-API

An Unreal Engine plugin that closes the loop between living neurons and real-time worlds.

UE-CL1-API is a closed-loop UDP interface between Cortical Labs' CL1 biocomputer and Unreal Engine — receiving living-neuron spikes as gameplay events and sending electrical stimulation back as feedback. It implements the closed loop from our NeurIPS 2025 paper Assembloid Agency on top of the official CL1 spike firehose, exposing Blueprint and C++ APIs for connection, stimulation, recording, spike read-back, and four-state game encoding/decoding.

It is backend-agnostic — the same plugin can drive a CL1 or an equivalent NEST / SNN / EEG stand-in over the same protocol — and ships with a bundled organoid simulator, so you can run the whole pipeline with no hardware.

Who we are

Team

Jenn Leung

Creative Technologist & Neuroarchitecture Researcher

Jenn Leung is a researcher and creative technologist working at the intersection of synthetic biology, real-time simulation, and living neural systems. She is a Senior Lecturer in Creative Technology & Design at University of the Arts London, a researcher at LifeFabs Institute, a biocomputing consultant at Callosum, and a Visiting Researcher at The Bartlett School of Architecture, UCL, working on the 100 Minds in Motion project combining EEG, eye-tracking, and movement data within agent-based simulation.

Her research currently focuses on developing Unreal Engine interfaces for living neurons and agent behaviour simulations. Two papers have recently been published in the MIT Antikythera Journal, and a paper on a UE-API for brain-on-a-chip platforms was presented at NeurIPS 2025. Since 2025, she has collaborated with the biocomputing start-up Cortical Labs to create human–synthetic biological intelligence visualizations.

Chloe Loewith

Bioethicist & Functional Genomics Researcher

Chloe Loewith is a researcher and writer working across synthetic biology, biomedical AI, and philosophy. She is a philosophy of technology researcher at NYU, and was previously a studio researcher at Antikythera's Cognitive Infrastructures Studio, a functional genomic bioethics researcher at SFU, and a graduate student at the University of Cambridge Centre for the Future of Intelligence.

Her work has appeared in the MIT Antikythera Journal, TANK Magazine, AI & Society, Biānjiè, HERVISIONS, PhysioNet, and the Whole Earth Redux. Her current research explores hybrid ecologies and co-evolutionary human–AI interaction, with a focus on cognitive assemblages, fluid ontologies, and distributed cognition.
